# Storytelling and Audiences

## Story arcs

| Arc | Shape | Use |
|-----|-------|-----|
| Before / after | Today's painful task -> the same task with the new design | Most product demos |
| Day in the life | One user through a sequence of moments | Platform or multi-feature work |
| Problem -> options -> recommendation | Two or three approaches, then the chosen one with reasons | Design reviews, trade-off decisions |
| Vision | Future state, then the first step toward it | Strategy, multi-quarter bets; frame clearly as vision |

Use a real user archetype grounded in research, with a name and a specific situation. Generic "the user" loses attention.

## Audience emphasis

| Audience | Cares about | Emphasise | Avoid |
|----------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Executives | Outcomes, cost, risk, timing, strategy | Problem size, impact estimate, the ask with options | Component details, edge cases |
| Product and design peers | Rationale, alternatives, research | Decisions and trade-offs, what you learned | Selling |
| Engineering | Feasibility, scope, states, unknowns | Edge cases, data needs, technical questions | Polish over substance |
| Customers | Their problem solved, ease, timing | Their workflow, their words | Internal names, roadmap promises |
| Sales / support | What to say, what changes for customers | Before/after, FAQs | Unconfirmed dates |

## Evidence

Pick one or two pieces, the strongest:
- 15-30 second research clip of a user struggling (with consent and anonymised as agreed)
- A metric (tickets, drop-off, time on task, revenue at risk)
- A quote, verbatim
- A test result ("7 of 8 participants completed the new flow; 3 of 8 the current one")

## Framing what's real

Say it plainly, once, without apologising:
- "This is a prototype. The screens are designed; the data and payments are mocked."
- "The AI suggestions here were written by us to show the experience; model quality is the open question."
- "This shows the ideal path; edge cases X and Y are not designed yet."
Unframed demos create commitments: stakeholders remember it working.

## Objections

| Objection | Response approach |
|-----------|-------------------|
| "Users won't do that" | Show the evidence; offer a test to settle it |
| "Competitor X does it differently" | Name the difference and why; acknowledge what they do well |
| "That's too much work" | Offer the smaller option you prepared |
| "Can we also add...?" | Note it, connect it to the decision, park it |
| Visual nitpicks in a concept demo | "Good catch; visuals are placeholder. Today we're deciding on the flow." |
